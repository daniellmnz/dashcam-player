Opening

Choose recording drive or folder takes a drive or any folder holding the recordings. Nothing is uploaded; everything stays on this computer. The folder is remembered, so later runs offer to reopen it. Choosing a folder also leaves a small marker file in it, so the export tools can find it again without asking. Given a whole drive, only the recorder's own folders are read — DCIM, SentryMode and Movies by default; Folders under Settings changes that list. A folder opened from inside the recordings is read as it stands.

The list

Recordings are grouped by day, newest first, and only the latest day is open to begin with. Sentry events are a group of their own, written in amber. A dot appears only where something is wrong with a recording; hover it for the reason.

Watching

The car writes all four cameras into one frame. Pick a single camera with the buttons, or watch the grid; In grid chooses which cameras appear in it. A straightened clip offers only Whole frame — it holds one camera, already flat. The playback speeds sit beside Play.

Settings

Trim takes a percentage off the top and bottom of every view — the sky, the car's own bodywork. Lens straightens the fisheye, and zoom enlarges or reduces the image. Whole frame · size draws the whole-frame view smaller, 100% down to 50%: it is the tallest thing here, and on a wide window it takes all the height it is given. The picture behind it stays at full resolution, so a snapshot is unaffected. Also here: playing straight on into the next recording, drawing the time onto the picture, the vehicle status strip, Folders — the top-level folders a drive is scanned for — and Reset settings. On the whole frame, and on a recording already straightened, the settings that cannot act on it are dimmed and say so; the rest still apply.

Saving

Snapshot writes the frame on screen as a JPEG. Export clip writes the camera you are watching out as its own file with ffmpeg. It shows you one frame of what it is about to write first — the camera as the tool will cut it, without the trim or lens correction, which are ways of looking at a recording and do not reach the file. What the question asks does: Rectify fisheye, Include the vehicle status strip, Quality, and — when straightening — whether to carry the top and bottom trim across, which is the one exception to that rule and is off unless asked for. Each box redraws the frame and changes what is written, together, so a setting caught at the last moment needs no second run, and every answer is remembered. Straightened, the frame is an approximation, close enough to judge the framing by. While the clip is being written the panel shows how far along it is and offers Cancel, which stops the tool itself rather than just leaving the page to stop watching. Mark In and Out first and it writes just that stretch instead. Tick Rectify fisheye beside it and the same button straightens the lens curve on the way: what it writes is named _rectified, listed under its own heading, and not straightened again when you play it. On a file that is already one camera the button does only that, since there is nothing left to split out of it. All are saved in the folder Dashcam and Sentry exports at the top of the drive or folder you opened, in sub-folders split, rectified and snapshots.

Sentry events

A sentry recording opens two seconds before it triggered, and its panel gives the time, the position on a map and the still the car saved, with its camera named in the corner where the recording says which one it is. Jump to the alert goes straight to the moment and switches to the camera it fired on, so what set it off is on screen rather than somewhere off it. Deleting a Sentry video file deletes its whole folder — the clip, the still and the data file.

Housekeeping

Tick recordings, or a whole day by its heading, and delete them from the drive. A recording the car never finished — power cut, USB drive removed — is rebuilt from the decoder settings of a healthy file beside it, automatically unless you turn that off.
